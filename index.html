Current research

M.Tech thesis — ongoing, June 2026 – present.

Distributed consensus in multi-robot systems, with collision & obstacle avoidance

Decentralized multi-robot consensus using weighted graph interactions and Laplacian-based coordination for distributed position agreement and collective field estimation.
Probabilistic collision-avoidance guarantees via Control Barrier Functions, process-noise modeling, and Cantelli's inequality — bounding pairwise safety violations rather than just hoping they don't happen.
Pointwise and mission-horizon safety bounds, extending the guarantee from individual robot pairs to the full multi-robot team, with a fixed minimum clearance.
Gradient-based field estimation integrated with distributed coordination for decentralized multi-robot motion, validated against empirical collision probabilities via Monte Carlo simulation.
A simplified illustration of the core idea — not a reproduction of thesis results. Agents reach consensus on a shared estimate while a barrier-function-style repulsion keeps every pair above a minimum clearance.
